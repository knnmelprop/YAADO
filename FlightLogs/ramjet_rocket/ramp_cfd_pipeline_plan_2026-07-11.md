## TL;DR
- **Run the existing "mesh rakiety" through a locally-built SU2 first**, treating that as the single highest-priority action: the sign-flip conflict (analytical +5 to +11 cal vs. old CFD −2.75 cal) is a CDR blocker, and one SU2 RANS-SST alpha-sweep at Mach 2.5 on the mesh you already have resolves it faster than any pipeline investment. But a *single coarse-mesh run cannot be declared CONFIRMED* — it must clear a mesh-quality gate (y+<1, orthogonality, prism layers) and a minimum 3-grid GCI study before it is allowed to arbitrate the conflict.
- **Build the durable automeshing pipeline second**, and split labor deliberately: SU2 as the open-source, reproducible-research solver wired into the repo, and PyFluent (license permitting) as the robust automeshing/production tool and cross-validation check — the two agree to ~0.2% of root chord on aerodynamic-center location for supersonic aircraft, but SU2's default JST scheme over-predicts drag by ~3–4% at Mach 2, so treat drag differences as a known systematic effect, not a bug.
- **Keep meshes/results out of git entirely** (DVC or Git LFS pointing to external storage; only configs/scripts in git), run CFD as a separate manual/nightly test tier with a deliberately coarse smoke-test case in CI, and mark every CFD output PROVISIONAL until it has a grid-convergence study and a cross-check against the Taylor–Maccoll (recovery 0.64) and DATCOM/Ackeret hand results.

## Key Findings

1. **The stability run is unblocked the moment SU2 builds locally.** SU2 builds from source with meson+ninja and requires only a C++ compiler and Python 3; MPI, swig, and mpi4py are needed only for parallel execution and the Python wrapper. The earlier cloud-sandbox failure ("no binary, no network to build the submodule") is a build-environment problem, not a solver problem — on a local machine with the CAD/mesh present it is a standard build.

2. **The correct SU2 physics setup for this regime is well-established.** Compressible RANS, `KIND_TURB_MODEL=SST` (Menter, with `SST_OPTIONS=V2003m` the current calibrated variant), y+<1 (SST resolves the viscous sublayer and does not use wall functions), Roe or AUSM-family flux with 2nd-order MUSCL and a Venkatakrishnan limiter, and adaptive CFL ramping. Characteristic-based far-field, adiabatic no-slip wall, and a symmetry plane are the standard marker set — this is exactly the ONERA M6 / turbulent-flat-plate tutorial pattern.

3. **A directly analogous published SU2-vs-Fluent supersonic study exists and is reassuring.** Viviani, Aprovitola, Dyblenko & Pezzella (*Aerospace* 2022, 9(8):411, DOI 10.3390/aerospace9080411) compared both codes on the *same* SST mesh for a Concorde-like aircraft and found the aerodynamic-center location agreed to ~0.2% of root chord at Mach 2 (verifying static margin "within 5% of the reference length"), with SU2 over-predicting drag by ~3–4% due to its JST scheme. This means a correctly-run SU2 job should *not* produce a stability sign flip — which strongly implies the old −2.75 cal thesis result is an artifact (mesh, reference-point, or moment-sign error), and reframes the SU2 run as an arbiter that will most likely vindicate the analytical positive static margin.

4. **Automeshing is a real choice between Gmsh (open, scriptable, but weak on 3-D boundary layers) and Fluent Meshing via PyFluent (robust watertight/fault-tolerant workflows, strong inflation layers, fully scriptable).** For a repeatable Python pipeline, PyFluent's watertight workflow is the production-grade automesher; Gmsh is the fully-open fallback and is excellent for 2-D/axisymmetric internal-duct cases.

5. **V&V doctrine is unambiguous:** the Roache/Celik GCI (Richardson extrapolation, ≥3 grids, safety factor Fs=1.25) is the ASME/AIAA-endorsed standard, and SU2 ships the EQUiPS module for turbulence-model epistemic uncertainty. A CFD result should be PROVISIONAL until it has GCI + a cross-check against the existing analytical results.

## Details

### RQ1 — Preparatory tasks for running the existing mesh in SU2

**Mesh-quality gate (run before any production solve).** Check the mesh against established thresholds before trusting it:
- **Orthogonal quality / non-orthogonality:** keep minimum orthogonal quality above ~0.1 and be cautious below that; poor orthogonality corrupts diffusion/gradient terms and drives divergence (Ansys guidance).
- **Skewness:** keep maximum skewness below ~0.9 (values >0.95 are generally unacceptable); tetrahedra are most sensitive.
- **Aspect ratio:** high aspect-ratio cells are fine and expected inside the boundary-layer prism stack (normal-to-wall stretching), but should be kept low (~<5) in the bulk/isotropic flow region.
- **Smoothness/growth:** cell-to-cell size transition should not exceed ~15–20% in the boundary-layer region.
- **y+ verification:** SST requires y+≈1 (target ≤1) because it resolves the sublayer; the standard boundary-layer construction is a first-cell height set for y+≤1, an expansion/growth ratio of ~1.1–1.2, and on the order of 10–15 cells across the boundary layer. Verify y+ *after* a first run (it is a solution-dependent output) and iterate on first-cell height if the target is missed.

**Far-field distance.** External-aero rule of thumb from published SU2 and airfoil/wing studies is a domain radius of ~10 root-chord lengths (semi-spherical/parabolic outer boundary); the missile-CFD literature (DeSpirito/Sahu grid-fin work at Mach 2.5) uses half-plane symmetry. For supersonic cases the downstream boundary must be far enough to contain the shock system; near-field sonic-boom work uses 0.5–1 body length for the very near field and 5–10 body lengths for extended near field.

**SU2 config for Mach 2.5 external aero:**
- `SOLVER=RANS`, `KIND_TURB_MODEL=SST`, `SST_OPTIONS=V2003m`.
- **Flux scheme:** Roe with `MUSCL_FLOW=YES` and `SLOPE_LIMITER_FLOW=VENKATAKRISHNAN` is the default recommendation; the AUSM family (including SLAU2 and the AUSM+-up variants implemented in SU2) is often more robust for strong shocks off the inlet cone and blunt-body carbuncle-prone regions. Recommend defaulting to Roe+MUSCL+Venkatakrishnan and keeping AUSM as the fallback if the bow/cone shock shows carbuncle or oscillation.
- **Shock robustness knobs:** `VENKAT_LIMITER_COEFF` (tune per mesh; reduce as mesh refines), and `ENTROPY_FIX_COEFF` (default 0.001; increasing it adds dissipation and can cure convergence stalls at the cost of sensitivity). Barth-Jespersen tends to fail to converge on shock cases; Venkatakrishnan is preferred.
- **CFL strategy:** `CFL_ADAPT=YES` with `CFL_ADAPT_PARAM=(factor-down, factor-up, CFL-min, CFL-max)` — e.g., start CFL ~5–10 and allow growth to several hundred; the adaptive law scales CFL by the residual ratio (CFL = CFL·(residual_old/residual_new)^power).
- **Boundary conditions:** `MARKER_FAR` (characteristic-based far-field), `MARKER_HEATFLUX` (adiabatic no-slip wall, q=0), `MARKER_SYM` if using a symmetry plane. SU2 supports supersonic-prescribed characteristic inlets and back-pressure outlets for the later internal-duct work.

**Common failure modes for missile/rocket geometries with large fins + shock forebody:** carbuncle/odd-even decoupling at the nose bow shock; limiter-induced convergence stall; residual hang from separated flow behind fins at high alpha; y+ blowout on the fin leading edges. Diagnose via residual history, y+ contour, and shock-region Mach contour; fix by switching Roe→AUSM/HLLC, raising the entropy fix, reducing CFL, or refining the fin-LE and shock regions.

### RQ2 — Automeshing pipeline architecture

- **Gmsh (open-source, Python API):** fully scriptable via the `gmsh` Python module (points→lines→surfaces→volumes, `extrude`, transfinite/structured constraints, `Mesh.MeshSizeFactor`). It has boundary-layer field capability but 3-D prism-layer generation on complex STL/CAD surfaces is historically fragile (documented failures where tets connect to the wall rather than to the prism cap). Best fit: 2-D/axisymmetric internal-duct meshes and simpler external cases; local refinement is manual via size fields (no built-in solution-adaptive refinement — that is done in the solver).
- **cfMesh / snappyHexMesh (OpenFOAM-native):** strong automatic hex-dominant meshing with inflation layers; usable outside OpenFOAM via conversion, and driveable from Python via wrappers like pyMeshFOAM (writes blockMesh/cfMesh/snappyHexMesh/Gmsh dictionaries and manages boundary layers). Adds an OpenFOAM dependency, so it is a secondary option.
- **Fluent Meshing via PyFluent (watertight & fault-tolerant workflows):** the most robust automesher for the cone+cylinder+swept-fin+boat-tail class. Watertight workflow for clean CAD, fault-tolerant for CAD needing cleanup (overlaps/holes). Full inflation-layer control (poly-hexcore volume fill), fully scriptable task-by-task in Python (`meshing_session.watertight()`, `import_geometry`, `add_local_sizing`, `create_volume_mesh`, then `switch_to_solver()`). This is the recommended production automesher **if a license is available** (confirmed 2026-07-11: academic Fluent license, local install on the team's Windows server).
- **Solution-adaptive refinement:** for shock regions, both SU2 and Fluent favor generating a reasonable base mesh then adapting to gradients rather than trying to pre-refine every shock; SU2 NICFD supersonic work notes grid adaptation is "key to capture the relevant flow features" economically.

### RQ3 — PyFluent specifics

- **Licensing/installation:** PyFluent is MIT-licensed but *requires a locally-installed, licensed Ansys Fluent* (supported: 2024 R2 SP05, 2025 R1/R2, 2026 R1+). It uses the `ANSYSLMD_LICENSE_FILE` environment variable and `AWP_ROOT<version>` to find the install; on Windows these must be set in the environment (system or user env vars) or passed to `launch_fluent`. Headless/CI automation is supported via `launch_fluent(mode=..., processor_count=...)` and via containerized Fluent (Docker/Podman) with the license server passed through the environment. **The team has an academic Fluent license installed locally on their Windows server — confirm the exact installed Fluent version against PyFluent's supported list above before relying on PyFluent in the pipeline; not otherwise assumed here.** PyFluent is *not* supported under a Fluent Pro license — confirm license tier, not just "academic," as part of the same check.
- **API shape:** `import ansys.fluent.core as pyfluent`; launch a meshing session, run the watertight workflow tasks, `switch_to_solver()`, set models/BCs, initialize (hybrid or FMG), iterate, and post-process (PyFluent-Visualization via PyVista/Matplotlib, or field data as NumPy arrays). The whole mesh→solve→post chain is one Python script. The package is split into ansys-fluent-core, ansys-fluent-parametric, and ansys-fluent-visualization.
- **Solver options for this regime:** density-based coupled solver for compressible/supersonic; Roe-FDS (general) or AUSM (better for hypersonic/strong-shock) inviscid flux; SST k-ω turbulence for external aero and internal duct; FMG initialization strongly recommended for large-gradient compressible cases; Convergence Acceleration for Stretched Meshes (CASM) as an aggressive convergence option (requires a good FMG start).
- **Combustor heat-addition without chemistry:** use an energy source term / simplified heat-source over the combustor cell zone (an "actuator-disk-style" or volumetric heat-source boundary condition) rather than a reacting-flow model — this represents Rayleigh-type heat addition without combustion chemistry (see RQ7).
- **Unsteady/URANS & hybrid RANS-LES for buzz/unstart:** Fluent supports URANS (dual-time), SAS-SST, DES-SST, and DDES. The inlet-buzz literature shows URANS with SST k-ω captures buzz frequency to within <0.2% of experiment in some cases, and DDES (hybrid RANS/LES, SA-based) matches the pressure-oscillation spectrum quantitatively — so URANS is the pragmatic first tool for buzz-onset, with DDES as the higher-fidelity escalation.

### RQ4 — SU2 vs PyFluent division of labor

The dominant industry pattern when both are available is **cross-validation**, which aligns perfectly with IADE's existing never-trust-a-single-method culture (CG sweeps, multi-method stability checks). Recommended split:
- **SU2** = reproducible open-source solver wired into the repo (config files are plain text and diff-able; pySU2 or subprocess integration; no license friction for CI). Primary for external-aero stability sweeps.
- **PyFluent/Fluent** = robust automeshing (watertight workflow) + production internal-duct/heat-addition runs + independent cross-check of SU2 force/moment coefficients.
- **Known systematic differences (critical for interpreting cross-checks):** Viviani/Aprovitola et al. (2022), same SST mesh, Concorde-like aircraft — aerodynamic-center location agreed to ~0.2% of root chord at Mach 2 and pitching moment about the AC to ΔC_Mac≈0.0002, but **SU2 over-predicted drag by ~3–4% at Mach 2 (~1.7% at Mach 1.5)** because its default 2nd-order JST central scheme is more diffusive than Fluent's Roe-FDS density-based upwind. The authors state the discrepancy "reflected mainly on drag computation performed with SU2," and that SU2 nonetheless "is capable to predict the correct shift of the aerodynamic centre at varying Mach numbers." Fluent used a density-based Roe-FDS solver; SU2 used JST with tuned dissipation coefficients. Practical rule for RamP: expect SU2 and Fluent to agree on *stability/moment/aero-center* (the CDR question) but expect SU2 to read higher on *drag*; do not treat that drag gap as an error. If drag accuracy matters, prefer an upwind scheme (Roe/AUSM) in SU2 — while noting the paper found SU2's upwind schemes *also* raised drag and introduced convergence issues.

### RQ5 — Verification & Validation practice

- **Grid convergence:** the Roache Grid Convergence Index, formalized in Celik, Ghia, Roache et al. (2008), "Procedure for Estimation and Reporting of Uncertainty Due to Discretization in CFD Applications," ASME J. Fluids Eng. 130(7):078001, is the standard. Minimum 3 systematically-refined grids (NASA-recommended refinement ratio r ≥ 1.3), compute observed order p, Richardson-extrapolate to zero grid spacing, and report GCI_fine = Fs·|ε21/φ1|/(r21^p − 1) with safety factor Fs=1.25 for a three-grid study (vs. Fs=3.0 for a two-grid study). Aerodynamic lift/drag GCI on the order of a few percent is typical; ensure the solution is in the asymptotic range.
- **Solution verification / turbulence UQ:** SU2's EQUiPS module (Eigenspace Perturbation, DARPA-funded; Mishra, Mukhopadhaya, Iaccarino & Alonso, *AIAA Journal* 2019, DOI 10.2514/1.J057187) runs a baseline + 5 perturbed simulations to bound epistemic turbulence-model uncertainty on quantities of interest — directly applicable to putting error bars on the static margin.
- **Validation as sanity check:** before trusting the SU2 stability number, cross-check against (a) the Taylor–Maccoll inlet recovery (0.64 at Mach 2.5), (b) the DATCOM-class buildup and Ackeret slender-body static margins, and (c) published missile-CFD benchmarks. The best directly-relevant benchmarks are DeSpirito & Sahu's ARL grid-fin studies at Mach 2.5: their ARL report (DTIC ADA383925) states "the aerodynamic coefficients were within 2.8–6.5% of the wind tunnel data … normal force coefficients on the individual grid fins were within 11%," and their AIAA Paper 2001-0257 reports computed vs. experimental axial force within 4–8% and normal force within 8–16%. These set realistic expectations for CFD-vs-test agreement on a finned supersonic body.
- **PROVISIONAL vs CONFIRMED definition for CFD (recommended):** a single coarse-mesh run = PROVISIONAL only. Promote to CONFIRMED only when: (1) the mesh passes the quality gate and y+<1 is verified; (2) residuals drop ≥4 orders (the intake-optimization literature standard); (3) a ≥3-grid GCI study shows asymptotic convergence with acceptable GCI on the static margin; and (4) the result is consistent with — or its disagreement with — the analytical cross-checks is explained. Only a CONFIRMED result may arbitrate the sign-flip conflict.

### RQ6 — Pipeline/repo integration & CI

- **Keep large files out of git:** the team's past binary-blob bloat is a solved problem with either **Git LFS** (simple, GitHub-native, pointer files) or **DVC** (better for experiment-style pipelines, selective download, remote on S3/Azure/GCS/NAS, records the command that produced each artifact). Recommendation: **DVC for meshes/results** (it version-controls the pipeline stage that produced them and only stores metadata in git), keep only configs/scripts/pydantic schemas in git. Never commit the SU2 submodule binaries or mesh files directly. **DVC remote target: pending — team's local NAS/server details (referenced informally as "melpropnas"/"melpropserver") have not been independently confirmed (hostname, protocol, DVC-compatible endpoint) as of this writing; treat as `[TBD-HUMAN]` in any generated DVC config rather than guessing a hostname.**
- **Wrapper choice:** two options — (a) `subprocess` calls to the `SU2_CFD` executable (simplest, robust, no swig/mpi4py build headache — the pySU2 wrapper build frequently fails on `mpi4py.i` discovery per SU2 issues #722/#1575), or (b) the pySU2 Python wrapper (in-memory driver control, needed only for co-simulation/adjoint/FSI, and requires building with `--enable-PY_WRAPPER` / `-Denable-pywrapper=true`). Recommendation: **default to subprocess for the stability pipeline**; reserve pySU2 for later coupled work. PyFluent uses its native session API.
- **Test tiering:** CFD is too heavy for the ~240-test pytest suite. Recommended pattern: (1) fast unit tests (config generation, pydantic schema validation, post-processing math) stay in the normal suite; (2) a **CI smoke test** runs a deliberately coarse/cheap case (e.g., a few-thousand-cell Euler or coarse-RANS run) to catch pipeline breakage on every commit without a production solve; (3) full production alpha-sweeps run in a separate **manual/nightly tier**. This mirrors standard heavy-job CI practice.

### RQ7 — Internal duct (inlet+nozzle) simulation prep

- **Mesh topology:** the oblique-shock train off the 42° spike and the terminal/normal shock demand streamwise refinement aligned with expected shock locations plus prism layers on the cowl and centerbody; the external-compression-intake literature (SALOME-meshed, ~220k nodes, 50 viscous layers, y+<1, AUSM+MUSCL+Venkatakrishnan) is a direct template. Use Riemann/characteristic supersonic inlet and back-pressure outlet BCs (SU2 supports both).
- **Heat addition without combustion chemistry:** the standard simplification is **Rayleigh flow** — frictionless constant-area duct with a lumped heat-addition term — implemented in CFD as a **volumetric energy source term** over the combustor zone (or an area-averaged/generalized Rayleigh representation that can also carry area change, friction, and wall heat loss, per the arXiv:2407.16287 generalized-Rayleigh treatment). This captures thermal-choking onset and back-pressure effects on the inlet without species/reaction transport — exactly matching the team's "combustor CFD out of scope" scoping. Set the combustor-exit total temperature to stay below the thermal-choking limit.
- **Validating the 1-D results:** a coupled or standalone internal RANS run gives a total-pressure-recovery number to compare against the Taylor–Maccoll 0.64; the intake-optimization paper found CFD TPR 0.885 vs. theoretical 0.922 for a Mach-2.5 external-compression intake, illustrating the ~4-point CFD-vs-theory gap to expect. The nozzle under-expansion (area ratio 1.317) from the Heiser & Pratt stream-thrust model can be checked against the CFD exit Mach/pressure.
- **Buzz/unstart prep:** because the inlet detaches below ~Mach 2.0–2.1, buzz-onset is the natural URANS study; start with axisymmetric/2-D URANS-SST (proven to capture buzz frequency to <0.2% in published work) before committing to 3-D DDES. SU2's own scramjet-unstart URANS thesis work (Purdue, inlet-isolator at ~Mach 5) shows SU2 itself can be used for this class if a Fluent license is unavailable.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Unblock and run the existing mesh (days, highest priority):**
1. Build SU2 locally from source (meson+ninja, GCC/Clang, Python 3; add MPI for parallel). Verify with a tutorial case (turbulent flat plate or ONERA M6).
2. Run the mesh-quality gate on "mesh rakiety" (orthogonality >0.1, skewness <0.9, boundary-layer growth ~1.1–1.2, ~10–15 BL cells). Fix or flag failures.
3. Write the SU2 config: RANS/SST-V2003m, Roe+MUSCL+Venkatakrishnan, adaptive CFL, MARKER_FAR / heat-flux wall / symmetry, Mach 2.5. Run alpha=0° first, confirm convergence (residuals ≥4 orders) and y+<1, then sweep 0–8°.
4. Extract Cm-vs-alpha and the aerodynamic center; compute static margin. Compare against +5 to +11 cal analytical and −2.75 cal old CFD. Mark result PROVISIONAL.

**Stage 1 — Confirm the arbiter (1–2 weeks):**
5. Run the ≥3-grid GCI study on static margin (Roache/Celik, r≥1.3, Fs=1.25). Optionally run EQUiPS for turbulence-model error bars.
6. Only when GCI is asymptotic and the y+/residual gates pass, promote to CONFIRMED and use it to resolve the sign flip. Given the Aprovitola/Viviani evidence, expect SU2 to confirm the *positive* analytical margin and expose the old −2.75 cal as an artifact — verify the old thesis's moment reference point and sign convention as part of the reconciliation.

**Stage 2 — Build the durable pipeline (weeks):**
7. Stand up repo hygiene first: DVC (or Git LFS) for meshes/results, configs/scripts only in git. Add `.gitignore`/`.dvcignore` entries for solver binaries and `.su2`/`.vtu` files. DVC remote target is `[TBD-HUMAN]` pending confirmed NAS/server connection details — do not invent a hostname.
8. Wrap SU2 via subprocess behind a pydantic-configured Python function; add a CI smoke-test tier (coarse case) + manual/nightly production tier, separate from the fast pytest suite.
9. Fluent license confirmed (academic, local install on the team's Windows server). Script the PyFluent watertight automesher (CAD→mesh) and a Fluent density-based Roe-FDS cross-check — but note these can only be *executed* on/from a machine with access to that Windows server install; anything built in a cloud/remote session is spec-correct but untested until run there. Confirm the exact installed Fluent version against PyFluent's supported list (2024 R2 SP05 / 2025 R1-R2 / 2026 R1+) and the license tier (not Fluent Pro) before relying on it in the pipeline.

**Stage 3 — Internal duct & buzz (later):**
10. Build the internal inlet→nozzle mesh (shock-aligned refinement, ~50 viscous layers, y+<1), Rayleigh volumetric heat-source in the combustor zone, compare TPR to Taylor–Maccoll 0.64 and nozzle exit to Heiser & Pratt.
11. Escalate to axisymmetric URANS-SST for buzz-onset near Mach 2.0–2.1; reserve DDES for high-fidelity spectral confirmation.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:** if SU2 *also* gives a negative static margin on a quality-gated, grid-converged mesh, the conflict is real and the geometry/CG assumptions must be re-examined (sweep CG per the PROVISIONAL convention). If drag is the quantity of interest, switch SU2 to an upwind scheme and expect Fluent to read lower. If the Fluent license situation changes, the pipeline stands on SU2+Gmsh alone with no loss to the stability arbitration.

## Caveats
- **Numeric guidelines are regime- and mesh-dependent.** y+<1, growth ratio 1.1–1.2, 10–15 BL cells, orthogonality >0.1, skewness <0.9, far-field ~10 chords, CFL ramps, and GCI safety factor 1.25 are widely-cited rules of thumb (Ansys/SU2/LEAP/Celik-Roache), not hard laws; tune to the actual RamP mesh and verify y+ as a solution output.
- **The SU2-vs-Fluent drag gap (~3–4%) is from one Concorde-like study**, not RamP; the *direction* (SU2 higher drag via JST) is a known solver characteristic, but the magnitude will differ for a fin-stabilized rocket. Aero-center/stability agreement (~0.2%) is the more transferable finding.
- **Buzz frequency accuracy (<0.2%) and grid-fin agreement (2.8–6.5% on integrated coefficients, up to 11–16% on individual-fin normal force)** come from specific published cases with their own geometries and are cited as feasibility evidence, not guarantees for RamP.
- **PyFluent license is confirmed to exist (academic, Windows server) but the exact version/tier compatibility with PyFluent is not yet independently verified** — treat as a required local check, not an assumption, before the PyFluent-side pipeline stages are trusted.
- **DVC/Git-LFS remote target ("melpropnas"/"melpropserver") is not documented anywhere in this project's existing docs** (verified by search 2026-07-11) — connection details (hostname, protocol, DVC-compatible endpoint) must come from the human before any pipeline config references a real remote.
- Some rules-of-thumb sources here are vendor blogs/forums (LEAP, CFD-Online, cfd.university) rather than primary literature; the primary anchors are the SU2 documentation, PyFluent documentation, Celik et al. (2008)/Roache, the EQUiPS AIAA paper (DOI 10.2514/1.J057187), the DeSpirito & Sahu ARL/AIAA grid-fin reports, and Viviani/Aprovitola et al. (2022) *Aerospace* 9(8):411 (DOI 10.3390/aerospace9080411). Where a specific numeric lacked an authoritative primary source it is flagged as a rule of thumb rather than a law; no license-cost figures were found and are treated as TBD.
